# ICLR submission draft: title, abstract, positioning, claims audit

Date: 2026-09-19. Status: working draft, nothing here is final. Sources: `experiments_halting_log.md` (§6, §6.1, §6.2), `results_loop_batch_2026-09-18.md`, `results_latent_batch2_2026-09-19.md`, `experiments_chain_batch1.md`, `paper_2604.07822_loop_think_generalize_summary.md`, `paper_2405.15071_grokked_implicit_reasoners.md`, `WALKTHROUGH.md` §3, and a read-only cross-check of `runs/halt_D*/final_eval.json` (§6 below). No experiment was run for this draft.

Terminology used throughout (define once in the paper, then keep fixed):

- **relation pair** (= skill pair): an ordered pair (r1, r2) of relations. n = 20 relations give n^2 = 400 ordered relation pairs.
- **k**: the number of distinct successor relations r2 that follow each relation r1 in the depth-2 training rows. The training relation graph is k-regular, so k·n of the n^2 ordered relation pairs are trained (k = 1: 20 / 400; k = 4: 80; k = 8: 160), each with all 500 start entities.
- **shallow data**: atomic facts, w2 rows (two independent one-hop questions) and d2 rows (depth-2 compositions). No training row has semantic depth > 2 unless a control says so.
- **unseen relation pair**: an ordered pair that never occurs in a d2 training row, although both relations occur in other pairs and every atomic fact is trained.
- **pending**: item 5 of the brief (RoPE variant, learned halting / random-budget baseline at k = 8, position-varied shallow programs). No outcome of a pending run is used anywhere below except as a placeholder.

Why T1 and T2.

- **T1** states exactly the scope that is supported today: a study of what is and is not learnable from shallow data, with the two mechanisms named in the subtitle. It stays true whichever way the pending runs go (a positive result enlarges the "can" side; the subtitle would then become "... and a Route to Depth from Two-Hop Supervision"). It cannot be confused with "Loop, Think, & Generalize": no verb triple, no "implicit reasoning in recurrent-depth transformers", and "shallow data" signals the different regime in the first three words.
- **T2** carries the conceptual contribution (identifiability) in a form that is robust to the pending runs: if 5(a) / 5(c) fail, the answer is "not with respect to addressing"; if they succeed, the answer is "when the local rule is the simplest one consistent with the data, or when positions vary". A declarative version ("Two Hops Do Not Identify the Loop") would be falsified by a success of 5(c), so the question form is deliberate. Cost: the word "identifiability" commits the paper to a formal statement (item 4 of §5).
- A good combination is T2 as main title with T1's subtitle: *When Do Two Hops Identify the Loop? Relation-Pair Diversity, Addressing Ambiguity, and the Limits of Shallow Supervision*.

Avoided on purpose: "solves", "achieves unbounded / infinite depth", "emergent" (finding 3 shows the apparent emergence is (1 - eps)^d amplification), comma-separated verb lists, and "implicit reasoning in recurrent-depth transformers" as a subtitle. T3's "but Not Depth" and T5 should be dropped if a pending run gives depth generalisation.

## 2. Abstracts

### Abstract A ("supportable today"; 229 words by `wc -w`, about 223 when typeset)

> Atomic facts and two-hop compositions are cheap supervision: n relations give at most n^2 relation pairs. We ask what a looped (weight-shared) transformer can and cannot acquire from such shallow data under final-answer supervision. Prior work on recurrent-depth transformers holds out atomic facts while training nearly every relation pair, and obtains depth extrapolation only with a curriculum over deeper chains; sparse relation pairs with depth ≤ 2 supervision remain open. With 500 entities and 20 permutation relations, we vary k, the number of successor relations following each relation in two-hop training. At k = 1 (20 of 400 pairs) the model fits training perfectly yet stays near chance on unseen relation pairs, answering r2(x) in 47-90% of cases, bypassing the bridge entity. At k = 4-8 the same model reaches 0.91-0.98 on unseen pairs within 10k updates, although continued training erodes this. Depth 3 still scores 0.00: models output r3(r1(x)) although r2(r1(x)) is decodable at the r2 position, an addressing rule that depth ≤ 2 data cannot distinguish from the correct one. The one-hop update itself is reusable: behind a local call interface, a 2-layer transformer learns an exact 10,500-entry table from the same data, certifying 120,000 test chains to depth 128. Error taxonomies, logit-lens readouts, causal patching and table replay thus separate a learnable, certifiable update from the autonomous control (relation selection, stopping) that shallow data leave unidentified.

Notes on Abstract A.

## 3. Positioning (introduction / related work)

**Versus Loop, Think, & Generalize (Kohli et al., arXiv 2604.07822, COLM 2026).** That paper shows that recurrent-depth transformers trained with final-answer supervision obtain two kinds of generalisation that standard transformers do not: systematic generalisation to two-hop queries built from atomic facts never used inside a training composition, and depth extrapolation by adding inference-time loops. We study the regime it leaves open, not its conclusions. First, its held-out unit is the atomic fact, not the relation pair: regenerating its two-hop data with the released generator, 39,954 of the 40,000 ordered relation pairs occur in training, every OOD test item uses a trained relation pair, and each (h, r1) prefix is followed by about 7.2 distinct r2; we hold out relation pairs and make the number k of trained successors per relation the independent variable (5% to 40% of pairs). Second, its depth extrapolation follows an easy-to-hard curriculum that adds 3-, 4-, ... hop data (up to 22 hops for the dynamic-recurrence model), and its Appendix J reports that a model that has generalised only to two hops does not extrapolate (generalisation ratio 1.0); we fix the training depth at ≤ 2, find the same null, and trace it to an addressing rule that such data cannot identify (the final position reads the first relation's position instead of the previous position). Third, its number of loops is an external test-time knob, optionally combined with a KL-and-entropy stopping heuristic applied at inference, with no learned halting and with "overthinking" limiting depth; whether stopping can be learned from final answers alone is one of our pending questions. Finally, its interpretability analysis (logit lens and activation patching of the bridge entity at the r1 position across three grokking phases) explains success on trained relation pairs, whereas ours targets the failures: an error-type taxonomy that names the shortcuts r2(x) and r3(r1(x)), logit-lens readouts showing that the correct intermediate is present but not read, and a finite-table certificate that separates the one-hop update from control.

**Versus Wang et al. 2024, "Grokked Transformers are Implicit Reasoners".** That work studies two-hop composition in standard (non-recurrent) transformers, where the controlling variable is the inferred-to-atomic ratio and OOD again means held-out atomic facts, with no relation pair held out (about 6.8 training instances per pair at ratio 7.2) and no depth beyond two; we vary relation-pair coverage instead, and at k = 4 we see the opposite temporal order to grokking, namely early generalisation to unseen relation pairs that continued training erodes.

**Versus ACT (Graves, 2016) and PonderNet (Banino et al., 2021).** We borrow only the form of the objective, a per-sample halting distribution q_t = p_t prod_{j<t}(1 - p_j) trained through the expected final-answer loss without halting labels (cost weight 0 in the main arm, 0.01 in a control arm), and use it to ask a different question: whether a stopping rule learned on depth ≤ 2 queries transfers to deeper chains; this is pending, and we claim nothing about adaptive computation beyond that test.

## 5. What is still missing for a credible ICLR submission (priority order)

1. **A positive depth result from depth ≤ 2 data, or a decision to submit as a diagnostic paper.** The stated goal is d → infinity from shallow data; today every autonomous model is at 0.00 for depth ≥ 3 and the only route to depth 128 is an external runner. Pending 5(a) / 5(c) decide this. Fix the success threshold and the reported checkpoint before reading the runs. Note that learned halting at depth ≥ 3 needs a run that is not yet submitted: the halting head on top of whichever [METHOD] works (the queued H / Hc / P runs use the NoPE backbone).
2. **De-confound and fill in the k axis on the looped model.** Hold exposures per fact and per row fixed across k (the P1 design), add k = 2, 3, 6 with 3 or more seeds, and report both the early peak and the 1M endpoint. Add the training-length axis with weight decay and dropout ablations to find out whether the erosion at k = 4 (and the milder one at k = 8) is a property of the recipe.
3. **Causal evidence for the addressing claim on the looped model.** Attention pattern of the final position per loop, and patching that swaps the state at the r1 position versus the r2 position at depth 3. Today the claim rests on output types and a logit lens; patching exists only for the 2-layer non-looped models.
4. **A formal statement of the two identifiability arguments.** (a) With k = 1, (x, r1) is a sufficient statistic for the d2 answer, so a bridge-free solution has zero training loss; say what k ≥ 2 changes. (b) On sequences of length ≤ 3, the addressing rules "previous position" and "first-relation position" agree on the whole training support; state the hypothesis class and which minimal data change (varied start positions with semantic depth ≤ 2) would separate them.
5. **A second world and scaling in n and E.** World seed 43 exists only for the 2-layer role-control setting. Needed: at least one more world for findings 1-2; n in {20, 50, 100} to learn whether the required k is constant (k·n = O(n) pairs) or grows with n; and non-permutation (partial, many-to-one) relations, where the r2(x) fallback is mostly undefined, as in the prior paper's two-hop world.
6. **Prior-paper baselines in our setting, and our manipulation in theirs.** Their recipes on our data: fixed R, dynamic R ~ clip(Poisson(4), 2, 8) (arm P, pending), and the KL-and-entropy stopping rule (already in `final_eval.json` as a diagnostic). A non-looped 8-layer model of matched FLOPs at k = 8, to say whether weight sharing matters for unseen relation pairs here. Conversely, thin out successor relations in their 2000-entity / 200-relation world to show that the pair-coverage effect is not an artefact of n = 20.
7. **One model family across the main findings.** Findings 1-2 use the 28.76M looped model, finding 3 a 1.62M 2-layer model, finding 4 a 5.14M latent executor at k = 1 only. Rerun the local-executor / external one-hop-calling diagnostic with the looped backbone, and either rerun the latent workspace at k = 8 or move it to the appendix.
8. **Statistics and bookkeeping.** Five seeds for headline numbers (seed variance is large: r2(x) share 0.47-0.90, erosion 0.47-0.79), pre-stated thresholds, best-by-validation and final checkpoints both reported, and the final 1M numbers written into `experiments_halting_log.md` §7, which is still empty.
